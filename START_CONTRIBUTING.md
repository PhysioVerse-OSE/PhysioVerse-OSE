**You do not need programming experience to contribute.**

## Five-Minute Contribution

1. Choose a project.
2. Click **Contribute**.
3. Complete the short GitHub form.
4. Add a public source when available.
5. Submit and join the discussion.

No cloning, Git commands, or coding are required.

## Community Discussion

For introductions, general questions, and ideas that span multiple projects, use [PhysioVerse Discussions](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/PhysioVerse-OSE/discussions).

## What to Submit

Choose a task that matches your expertise. Provide what is known and use **Not reported**, **Not applicable**, or **Unknown** explicitly when needed. Do not infer missing study details or create example results to fill a record.

| Your goal | What to provide | Start a contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Make a public MPS dataset easier to find | Source accession or DOI, model and tissue, available measurements, data access link, and a short explanation of relevance. | [MPS Data Commons](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-data-commons/issues/new?template=community-contribution.yml) |
| Improve minimum metadata | Proposed field, definition, applicable models, reason for inclusion, suggested requirement level, and existing standard or vocabulary when available. | [MPS Minimum Metadata Standard](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-minimum-metadata/issues/new?template=community-contribution.yml) |
| Describe or compare models | Intended use, source identity and version, tissue and cell source, architecture, culture and device conditions, endpoints, evidence sources, and missing information. | [MPS Model & System Descriptors](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-model-system-descriptors/issues/new?template=community-contribution.yml) |
| Contribute validation evidence | Defined application, comparison or reference, measured endpoints, study design, repeatability or cross-laboratory evidence, results with uncertainty when available, and limitations. | [MPS Validation Framework](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-validation-framework/issues/new?template=community-contribution.yml) |
| Connect multiple data modalities | Available files, original sample and study identifiers, relationships between measurements, time references, processing history, and access conditions. | [MPS Multimodal Data Standard](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-multimodal-data-standard/issues/new?template=community-contribution.yml) |
| Make a public protocol easier to reuse | Protocol link and version, model and cell source, medium and matrix, duration, quality-control criteria, and related publication or dataset links. | [MPS Protocol Commons](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-protocol-commons/issues/new?template=community-contribution.yml) |

You can also review definitions, improve documentation, check source links, or explain what prevents reuse. These are useful scientific contributions even when they do not involve code.

### Nominate a Dataset or Submit Data Files?

**Nominate a public dataset on GitHub:** share a source link, accession, DOI, and its scientific context through the [Data Commons form](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-data-commons/issues/new?template=resource-nomination.yml). A nomination is a resource recommendation, not a raw-data deposit.

**Submit data files:** use the [PhysioVerse submission pathway](https://physioverse.org/submit-data) and its access instructions. Do not upload large raw datasets, confidential information, or patient-identifiable information into public Issues or pull requests.

The [Find Data page](https://physioverse.org/find-data) distinguishes public study-metadata discovery from signed-in access to contributor-submitted data. A discoverable study record does not guarantee that its underlying data are openly downloadable.

## Standards and Benchmarking

**Help shape shared standards:** start with [minimum metadata](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-minimum-metadata), [model descriptors](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-model-system-descriptors), [experimental conditions](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-experimental-conditions), [assays and endpoints](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-assay-endpoint-dictionary), or [multimodal data linkage](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-multimodal-data-standard). Suggest mappings to an existing standard or vocabulary before proposing a new term.

**Explore benchmarking:** [Toward Standardization](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/toward-standardization) includes an initial [lung transcriptomic fidelity application](https://physioverse.org/tools/toward-standardization). Interpret its metrics within the stated reference and use case, not as a universal model-quality score.

**Contribute evaluation evidence:** the [MPS Reference Atlas](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-reference-atlas), [MPS Validation Framework](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-validation-framework), and [MPS Reproducibility Commons](https://github.com/PhysioVerse-OSE/mps-reproducibility-commons) welcome reference nominations, intended-use evidence, and reproducibility studies.

Community working drafts and evidence reviews complement formal standards development. They are not formal standards, certification, or regulatory approval.

## Two Ways to Contribute

### 1. Share Expertise Through an Issue Form

Sign in to your own GitHub account, choose a project, and open its contribution form. Add your source and explanation, submit, and keep the URL. No coding, local software, or write access to the repository is needed for this route when public Issues are available.

### 2. Propose a Document Change Through a Pull Request

1. Sign in to your own GitHub account and open the project file you want to improve.
2. Select the pencil or edit option. Follow GitHub's prompt to fork the repository when you do not have write access.
3. Make a focused change to documentation, a working draft, a resource record, or a template. Preserve source attribution and existing identifiers.
4. Choose a descriptive commit message and propose the change on a new branch. Do not commit directly to the project's default branch.
5. Open a pull request to the original **PhysioVerse-OSE** repository. Check that the base repository and base branch are correct.
6. Explain what changed, why it is useful, and which Issue or public sources support it. Review the diff before submitting.
7. Respond to review comments in the pull request. A maintainer can merge the change after review.

Keep each proposed change focused on a useful, source-supported improvement.

## What Happens After You Contribute?

**Submit → review → clarify or revise → incorporate accepted material → retain attribution and source links.**

A maintainer reviews the contribution and may ask for clarification, supporting evidence, or a narrower scope. Accepted material can be incorporated into the appropriate project resource. A closed or deferred proposal should retain its discussion and explanation. Acceptance depends on the scope and supporting evidence.

Questions, supporting information, and revisions should remain in the relevant GitHub thread.

## Credit for Your Contribution

Use your own GitHub account. Accepted scientific input can be acknowledged through its Issue or Discussion link and the associated project record, whether or not it includes code.

GitHub's repository contributor graph is separate from those acknowledgments. It generally depends on eligible commits incorporated into the default branch and an author email associated with the contributor's account. Opening an Issue, posting a Discussion, or receiving collaborator access alone does not guarantee inclusion in that graph. See [GitHub's contributor documentation](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/viewing-activity-and-data-for-your-repository/viewing-a-projects-contributors).

We aim to preserve genuine attribution when material is incorporated, including source links and the contributor's own commits where applicable. Participation alone does not imply authorship, endorsement, or consensus.
